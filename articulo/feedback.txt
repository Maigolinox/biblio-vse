Review: Mechanistic LLM Control for ISO/IEC 29110 Com- pliance: A Structural Isomorphism Framework
Venue: IEEE Access (software-engineering scope) · Roughness: journal · Reviewer:
Claude (review-research-article skill) · Date: 2026-06-02


Summary
The paper proposes a framework for auditing ISO/IEC 29110 process compliance in software repositories with open-weight LLMs, under the constraint that real indus- trial repositories cannot be released for privacy reasons. It makes two coupled contri- butions: (1) a “structural isomorphism” procedure that builds a synthetic benchmark (Biblio-VSE, N=20 artifacts) constrained to be distributionally similar to a single pri- vate industrial repository on a handful of code/text metrics; and (2) a “tripartite” LLM pipeline that combines zero-shot structured prompting, retrieval-augmented genera- tion (RAG) over the ISO/IEC 29110 normative text, and contrastive activation steer- ing. Four 7–9B open-weight models are evaluated across eight configurations. The headline result is F1=0.880 (Phi-3.5-mini, zero-shot + steering). To the authors’ con- siderable credit, the paper explicitly frames all results as exploratory and hypothesis- generating: no pairwise comparison survives Holm–Bonferroni correction, and the limitations are stated unusually candidly.


Strengths
•	Unusually honest statistical reporting. The paper reports raw and Holm- corrected p-values (Table 5), states plainly that no result survives correction at α=0.05, and repeatedly frames findings as exploratory. This is rarer than it should be and is the single most commendable feature of the manuscript.
•	A genuinely real problem. Privacy-constrained compliance auditing for VSEs is a real barrier (§I–II), and the motivation that industrial repositories cannot be published for benchmark construction is well argued and well cited [31]–[34].
•	The regex baseline is included and honestly interpreted. Many papers would have hidden a baseline that achieves F1=1.000. The authors instead fore- ground it (§V-E, §VII-A) and use it to interrogate their own construct validity. This is good scientific practice.
•	Decomposition of the pipeline is clean. The eight-configuration factorial (baseline / ZS / RAG / steering and their combinations, §IV-E) lets the reader see independent and combined effects, and the per-artifact error analysis (Table 7) is informative about where models fail (TESTING, governance source files).
•	Reproducibility scaffolding is largely present. Public GitHub repo, dataset JSON, per-artifact outputs, extracted steering vectors, normative corpus, and a complete hardware/software table (Table 4) are provided. The air-gapped design (local embeddings + local inference) is described concretely.
 
•	Air-gapped, no-fine-tuning steering is practically attractive. Extracting a steering vector from two contrastive prompts with no labeled data and no fine- tuning is a sensible fit for the resource-constrained VSE setting the paper tar- gets.


Weaknesses
The weaknesses below are organized by the rubric I inferred for this paper type (an empirical applied-ML / software-engineering systems paper): (1) construct validity of the benchmark, (2) statistical conclusion validity, (3) selection/overfitting bias in the method, (4) confounds in the steering contribution, (5) termi- nological overclaiming, (6) reproducibility of the privacy claim, (7) signifi- cance/contribution. At journal bar, several of these are first-order.

1.	Construct validity: the benchmark is largely solvable without semantic reasoning
The regex baseline achieves perfect F1 (§V-E). The authors interpret this fairly — a spectrum of regex-solvable vs. semantically-hard meta-rules (§VII-A) — but the con- sequence is more damaging to the central thesis than the paper admits. The mo- tivating claim is that naive LLMs fail at normative compliance reasoning and that the proposed pipeline supplies that reasoning. Yet on this benchmark, only two of nine meta-rules (GOVERNANCE, TESTING) are described as genuinely requiring se- mantic understanding, and the universally-failed artifact (SMP-PRUEBAS-NEG) fails for a prompt-design reason (no explicit TESTING criterion in the prompt), not for lack of model capability. With N=20 artifacts and roughly two semantically-hard rules, the benchmark cannot distinguish “the model reasons about norms” from “the model pattern-matches lexically distinctive markers.” The paper needs adversarial artifacts that contain compliance-related keywords without satisfying the policy (the authors propose exactly this as future work in §VII-A — at journal bar it should be present, not deferred).

2.	Statistical conclusion validity: N=20 cannot support the claims
With 20 artifacts, every headline comparison is underpowered, and the authors say so (§VI-B, §VII-D). But the implications propagate further than the paper acknowledges:
•	The “global maximum” F1=0.880 has 95% CI [0.70, 1.00] (Table 5). That inter- val includes the baseline of essentially every model. The point estimate is not separable from noise.
•	The two raw-significant results (Qwen2.5 ZS p=0.0455; Phi-3.5 ZS+Steering p=0.0348) both die under Holm correction (adj. p=1.000 and 0.940). The paper correctly reports this, but the narrative throughout §V–VI is still organized around these point estimates (“best-performing combination,” “global maxi- mum,” “Gemma-2 peaks at…”) in a way that overstates what the data licenses. A reader skimming the results section will retain conclusions the statistics do not support.
 
•	§VI-B states that swapping a single artifact shifts F1 by ~0.05, “larger than the gap between the best and second-best configurations.” This is the real finding of the experiment, and it argues that the per-configuration F1 rankings should not be reported as findings at all, only as illustrations.
This is not fatal for a proof-of-concept framing, but it is in tension with publishing per- model “winning configurations.” Either the artifact set must grow to the N≈100–200 the paper itself estimates (§VI-B, §VII-D), or the results must be reframed so that no configuration is named as best.

3.	Selection/overfitting bias: headline numbers are in-sample
The intervention coefficient α=0.8 and the per-model intervention layer were selected on the same 20 artifacts used for evaluation (α “selected through pilot experimenta- tion on the same 20 evaluation artifacts,” §IV-D; layer chosen at ~50% depth with “no systematic sweep”). The paper flags this (§IV-D: “Because α=0.8 was selected on the same data used for evaluation, the reported F1 values may be optimistic”), but the disclosure does not neutralize the problem: the reported steering F1 values are best understood as fit on the evaluation set, not as held-out performance. Given that steering is sold as the most consistently beneficial intervention, this bias falls pre- cisely on the paper’s main contribution. A held-out split (even leave-one-out over the 20 artifacts, or a second synthetic repository for hyperparameter selection) is needed before the steering gains can be reported as real.

4.	The steering contribution carries an uncontrolled language confound
The contrastive anchor prompts (p⁺/p⁻) are in English while the system prompt and all evaluation artifacts are in Spanish (§IV-D, Fig. 8 caption). The paper names this as an uncontrolled variable (§VII-A). For the paper’s flagship mechanism this is a serious confound: the extracted vector v_norma may encode a language/persona direction as much as a “compliance” direction. Combined with the §VII-C concern that p⁺/p⁻ con- flate “strict auditor persona” with “compliance semantics,” the steering vector’s in- terpretation is doubly underdetermined. At minimum the paper should report a same- language ablation; without it, the claim that steering encodes a “compliance/violation direction” is unsupported.

5.	Terminological overclaiming: “mechanistic” and “isomorphism”
Two central terms are stronger than what is delivered.
•	“Mechanistic.” The method is contrastive activation addition (a standard representation-engineering / activation-steering technique). There is no mechanistic-interpretability analysis — no circuits, no causal tracing, no component-level attribution. “Mechanistic interpretability” is invoked in §III-C and the title, but the work is inference-time activation steering. Recommend retitling to “activation steering” / “representation engineering” to match the contribution and avoid reviewer pushback on misuse of “mechanistic.”
 
•	“Structural isomorphism.” What is defined (Eqs. 2–4) is bounded difference of means and variances on a few marginal metrics — a distributional similar- ity, and an admittedly partial one (the Fog Index threshold fails, |Δμ|=2.15 > ε_F=1.0; AST depth and Lexical Density have no thresholds at all). “Isomor- phism” implies a structure-preserving bijection; the paper demonstrates approx- imate agreement on four univariate summaries from a single source repository. The authors honestly downgrade to “partially isomorphic under selected observ- able metrics” (§IV-B, §VII-A), but the title and framing still lead with “isomor- phism.” The mapping f: D_private → D_synthetic is asserted but never actually exhibited or learned (learning it is listed as future work). Recommend softening the framing throughout to “distributionally constrained synthetic benchmark.”

6.	The privacy claim cannot be independently verified, and rests on n=1 repository
The synthetic-vs-private similarity (Fig. 5) is computed against a single private repos- itory whose raw data “will not be released” (Data Availability). Only aggregate distri- butional statistics from that one organization are shown. This makes the core privacy- preservation contribution unfalsifiable by reviewers: there is no way to check whether Biblio-VSE actually preserves the governance topology of real repositories, because
(a) only one private repo was used, and (b) even its artifacts are unavailable.  The
§VII-A admission that “unmeasured structural dimensions — inter-artifact coupling, organizational workflow complexity, approval-chain topology — represent additional limitations” concedes that the governance-topology preservation that motivates the whole approach is precisely what is not measured. The isomorphism is demonstrated on text/code surface metrics, not on the compliance structure the paper says matters.

7.	Significance relative to the regex baseline
Given (1), the practical takeaway is uncomfortable: on the released benchmark a handful of regexes match or beat every LLM configuration at zero cost and with per- fect interpretability. The paper needs to make a positive case for when the LLM pipeline is expected to beat regex on real repositories (presumably when evidence is natural-language and non-templated), and ideally show at least one artifact class where this is empirically true. As written, a skeptical reader concludes the LLM ma- chinery is not yet justified on the evidence presented.


Detailed comments by section
•	Abstract. Accurately previews the exploratory framing — good. But “Activation steering is the most consistently beneficial single intervention across all four architectures” is a claim the corrected statistics do not support; recommend “showed the most consistent positive direction of effect, though no comparison survived multiple-comparison correction.”
•	§I Introduction / Fig. 1. Clear. The four contributions are well delineated. Contribution 2 (“privacy-preserving synthetic benchmark construction based on
 
structural isomorphism”) should be softened per Weakness 5–6.
•	§II Literature method / Figs. 2–3. A full Kitchenham+Wohlin SLR protocol is described (database search, PRISMA-like screening, snowballing).  This is disproportionate machinery for what ultimately yields a fairly standard related-work section, and no SLR artifacts are reported (no count of records screened/included, no PRISMA flow numbers, no inclusion table beyond Table 1’s five themes). Either report the SLR outputs (counts at each stage) or downgrade §II from “we conducted an SLR” to “we surveyed related work,” because as written it claims a method whose results are not shown.
•	§IV-A Meta-rules (Table 2). The nine meta-rules and their mapping to ISO/IEC 29110 work products are a reasonable operationalization. φ is described infor- mally; the actual logical predicates would help reproducibility.
•	§IV-B Isomorphism (Eqs. 2–4, Figs. 5–6). As above, the thresholds ε=0.5, δ=0.25, ε_F=1.0 are asserted without justification (“no formal thresholds were defined for AST depth or Lexical Density”). Where do ε and δ come from? Two of four metrics have no threshold and one fails its threshold; only V(G) passes both. Reporting four metrics and having one pass cleanly is thin support for “isomorphism.”
•	§IV-C / Fig. 5 / Table 3. N=20 (11 compliant, 9 violations). The class bal- ance should be stated when discussing F1, because F1 on an 11/9 split with recall-maximizing models inflates easily (see below). The Git-branch injection of violations (x⁻) is a sensible construction but means violations are synthetically distinctive, reinforcing Weakness 1.
•	§IV-D Pipeline / Figs. 7–9. Technically clear. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 for RAG, top- k=4, ≤1500 chars — fine. The activation-steering math (Eqs. 5–6) is standard CAA and typechecks. Layer selection at ~50% depth with no sweep is the largest unaddressed sensitivity (acknowledged §VII-B).
•	§V Results / Figs. 11–15, Tables 5–6. The heatmap and forest plot are the right visualizations. Please add the class-prior / majority-class F1 as a reference line: a trivial “always compliant” classifier on the 11/9 split scores precision 0.55, recall 1.00, F1≈0.71. Several reported F1 values (e.g., baselines ~0.70) are at or barely above this floor, which the reader needs to calibrate against. Phi-
3.5 Exp 6 (TP=11, TN=5, FP=3, FN=0) achieves F1=0.880 by never missing a positive while the dataset has 11 positives — i.e., the headline is recall-driven on a positive-heavy set, not evidence of balanced discrimination.
•	§V-E Regex baseline. Excellent that it’s here. Move part of the §VII-A dis- cussion up into §V-E so the F1=1.000 result is contextualized at the point of reporting.
•	§VI Discussion / Figs.  16–17.  The “Important caveat” paragraph in §VI-B is the most valuable paragraph in the paper and should arguably be promoted earlier. The radar/F1-delta charts are fine but risk over-interpretation given the CIs.
•	§VII Threats to validity. Genuinely thorough and self-aware — among the best parts of the manuscript. The construct, internal, external, and conclusion sub- sections each name real problems. The paper would be stronger if some of these threats were addressed rather than enumerated (esp. the held-out selection of α and layer, and the same-language steering ablation).
•	§VIII Conclusion. Appropriately hedged.
 
•	References. Current and relevant (2024–2025 preprints for LLaMA/Mistral/Phi/Qwen/Gemm activation steering [40]–[42], data-leakage [38]–[39]). Citation [42] “Inference-
time intervention” and [40] “Representation engineering” are correctly attributed. No hallucinated citations detected on inspection. Mixed but acceptable formatting.
•	Production/cosmetic. The PDF still carries IEEE Access template place- holders: “Date of publication xxxx,” “Digital Object Identifier 10.1109/AC- CESS.2017.DOI,” and “VOLUME 4, 2016” running footer, despite citing 2025 work. Author affiliation line has a typo (“Boulder” appears in the Tec de Monterrey address). Clean these before submission. The first author’s surname appears as both “TERRÓN-MACIAS” (byline) and “Terón-Macias” (running header) — unify.

Questions for the authors
1.	α and the intervention layer were tuned on the 20 evaluation artifacts. What is the steering F1 under leave-one-out (or on a second held-out synthetic reposi- tory)? Without this, how should the reader read the steering gains as anything but in-sample fit?
2.	Can you report a same-language (Spanish p⁺/p⁻) steering ablation? How much of v_norma’s effect survives when the language mismatch is removed?
3.	The regex baseline gets F1=1.000. On which specific artifact classes, if any, does any LLM configuration beat a sufficiently engineered regex? If none on this benchmark, what is the empirical case for the LLM pipeline?
4.	The isomorphism is verified on V(G), Fog, AST depth, and Lexical Density from a single private repository — and Fog fails its threshold. Why these four metrics, where do ε=0.5 / δ=0.25 / ε_F=1.0 come from, and why should distributional agreement on these marginals imply preservation of governance topology (the property you argue actually matters)?
5.	Given §VI-B’s finding that flipping one artifact moves F1 by ~0.05 — larger than the best-vs-second-best gap — on what basis do you report per-model “winning configurations” rather than declaring the rankings indistinguishable?
6.	What is the majority-class / “always compliant” F1 on your 11/9 split, and how do the baseline F1≈0.70 results compare to it? Several point estimates appear close to the trivial floor.
7.	§II describes a full Kitchenham+Wohlin SLR. Can you report the screening counts at each stage (records identified / screened / included)? Otherwise, why frame §II as an SLR rather than a narrative survey?
8.	The title claims “Mechanistic.” What mechanistic-interpretability evidence (cir- cuits, causal mediation, component attribution) distinguishes this from standard activation steering, beyond choosing a layer?


Recommendation
•	Score: Major revision (on a reject / major / minor / accept scale). The prob- lem is real, the engineering is sound, and the honesty is exemplary — but at journal bar the headline empirical claims are not supported: N=20, in-sample hyperparameter selection, an uncontrolled language confound on the flagship mechanism, and a benchmark a regex solves perfectly. None of these is fatal to a proof-of-concept paper, but the current framing repeatedly states conclu- sions (best configuration, global maximum, “most beneficial intervention”) that the statistics explicitly cannot support.
•	Confidence: 4 / 5.
•	One-line justification: Honest, well-motivated proof of concept undermined by a benchmark too small and too regex-solvable to license its conclusions; revise to (a) enlarge/adversarialize the benchmark or fully reframe as exploratory with no named winners, (b) select steering hyperparameters out-of-sample, and (c)
 
soften “mechanistic”/“isomorphism.”
